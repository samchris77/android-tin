Match the loudness

Step 2 / 2

Adjust until the tone feels equally loud as your tinnitus. Most people land between 5–15 dB SL — the perception is real, but quieter than people expect.

+8 dB SL Above your hearing threshold at 6.2 kHz.
Match

Why we measure this

Loudness matching is for understanding, not for tracking progress. Distress (TFI) is what improves with habituation — most people's measured loudness barely changes even when they feel much better.

Experimental: notched sound therapy We can generate a broadband noise with a notch at 6.2 kHz. Evidence for notched therapy is low — frame this as something to try, not a primary treatment.

Should I try rTMS?

?
Honest answer · not optimised for engagement

It's not a cure, and on its own it doesn't work.

rTMS, tDCS, and VNS show short-term benefit in some studies, but long-term outcomes are not statistically significant versus sham. They're worth considering only as adjuncts to TRT or CBT, never standalone.

rTMS Low–moderate

Small short-term effect on distress. Long-term: not significant. Combine with TRT/CBT.

Evidence strength2 / 5
Commercial 알파-스팀 device Watchlist

Marketed for tinnitus without published RCT evidence. Korean manufacturer cites general anxiety studies, not tinnitus outcomes.

Evidence strength0 / 5 for tinnitus

Take to your clinician

  • Has this device been studied for tinnitus, or only for other conditions?
  • What's the long-term (≥ 6 month) data?
  • Is a sham-controlled trial available?
