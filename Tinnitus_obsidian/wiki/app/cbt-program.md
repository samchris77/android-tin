# App CBT Program Regimen

## Overview
This feature offers a structured, long-term guided psychological intervention directly on the user's phone. Based heavily on the protocols validated in the Kalmeda trial (Walter et al., JMIR 2025), the focus is on a progressive behavioral therapy curriculum that users engage with daily.

## Structure and Progression
The program is built as a multi-level curriculum (e.g., 5 levels with 9 steps each, or an 8-week intensive daily structure).

### Phase 1: Psychoeducation (Directive Counseling)
Users start with short, high-quality educational modules (videos or interactive cards).
- Anatomy of the auditory system.
- The neurophysiological model of tinnitus (explaining central gain and the Triple Network Model in layman's terms).
- Reassuring the user: "Tinnitus is a false alarm, not a danger signal."

### Phase 2: Cognitive Restructuring
- **Thought Diaries**: Users log their negative thoughts regarding tinnitus (e.g., "I will never enjoy silence again").
- The app uses an automated or AI-assisted chatbot to challenge these cognitive distortions and help the user reframe the thought (e.g., "I am currently struggling to focus, but this will pass, and I can use background sound for now").

### Phase 3: Coping Skill Acquisition
Interactive exercises for:
1. Guided breathing (4-7-8, box breathing).
2. Deep progressive muscle relaxation.
3. Attention refocusing tasks (shifting focus away from the auditory domain to somatic or visual domains).

## Tracking and Retention
- **Self-Assessment Check-ins**: Integration of the THI (Tinnitus Handicap Inventory), VAS (Visual Analog Scale) for loudness/distress, and PHQ-9 for depressive symptoms every 2-4 weeks.
- Graphing these visual results forms a powerful adherence incentive, showing users their progressive drop in distress.

## Target Engagement
We aim for users to spend approximately 10 minutes a day actively in the CBT module. As shown in recent clinical literature, consistency over durations of 6-9 months yields significant, clinically important differences resulting in habituation of the tinnitus percept.
