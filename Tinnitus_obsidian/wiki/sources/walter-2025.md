# Continuous Improvement of Chronic Tinnitus Through a 9-Month Smartphone-Based Cognitive Behavioral Therapy: Randomized Controlled Trial (Walter et al., 2025)

- **Type:** RCT (Randomized Controlled Trial)
- **Year:** 2025
- **Population / N:** 187 patients with chronic tinnitus (> 3 months duration)
- **Intervention:** 9-month app-based CBT (Kalmeda App) without physical clinical visits
- **Comparator:** 3-month waiting list control, followed by active therapy
- **Primary outcome:** Tinnitus Questionnaire (TQ) sum score variance
- **Result:** Significant decrease in TQ score: -12.49 points after 3 months, and -18.48 points after 9 months.
- **Risk of bias / quality:** Moderate. 9-month follow-up is unusually long for a digital therapeutic. However: comparator is a waitlist control (not active treatment), blinding is impossible by design, and the post-waitlist crossover period complicates pure intention-to-treat interpretation. No sham-controlled arm. Effect size (d=1.38) should be interpreted relative to waitlist, not active treatment.

## Key findings
- TQ score consistently declined across the treatment period. While traditional manual treatments see regressions, this app achieved continued improvement through month 9.
- Secondary endpoints like perceived stress (PSQ-20) and depression (PHQ-9) also experienced significant, clinically relevant improvements.
- No severe adverse events were reported, confirming internet/app based CBT as an extremely safe digital intervention.
- Proves that guided/in-person therapy is not strictly necessary for achieving significant, moderate-to-large effect sized habituation outcomes. Effect size (Cohen's d) reached 1.38 at 9 months.

## Quotes worth preserving
> "The data in our study provide evidence of statistically significant, clinically relevant, and continuous benefits of an app-based CBT intervention in patients with chronic tinnitus." (Abstract)
