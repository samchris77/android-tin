# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) for Tinnitus

## TL;DR
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) for tinnitus aims to reduce the distress, anxiety, and depression associated with the sound, rather than changing the sound itself. Currently, it holds the highest level of clinical evidence for treating chronic tinnitus and is the primary psychological intervention recommended. Long-term studies show that it can be successfully delivered via an automated smartphone app format.

## What it is
CBT is a psychotherapeutic approach that identifies and challenges negative, maladaptive thoughts related to tinnitus (e.g., "This sound is making me lose my mind", "I can never sleep again"). Procedures include cognitive restructuring, attention refocusing techniques, relaxation training, mindfulness, and behavioral modification (such as exposure to silenced environments).

## Theoretical basis
CBT functionally targets the **Salience Network** and **Central Executive Network**. See [[wiki/clinical/triple-network-model]]. By reshaping how the brain evaluates the tinnitus signal, the patient can consciously suppress the emotional reflex, leading to habituation of reaction. See [[wiki/clinical/habituation]].

## Evidence
- **Strength of evidence:** high
- **Key trials:** 
  - NEJM 2018 Clinical Guidelines recommend CBT explicitly because systematic reviews find positive, durable effects on quality of life and distress compared to no-treatment controls.
  - JMIR 2025 (Walter et al.) showed 187 patients receiving 9-month app-based CBT had an effect size of 1.38 at 9 months, significantly dropping their Tinnitus Questionnaire (-18.48) and PHQ-9 (depression) scores.
- **Limitations of the evidence base:** CBT does not reliably reduce the subjective loudness matching of the tinnitus. Studies are also frequently unblinded since participants know they are receiving therapy.
- **Korean HTA cross-finding ([[wiki/sources/neca-2022-trt]]):** in the 2022 NECA reassessment of TRT, CBT (categorized as 인지치료, including coping training, classical CBT, and ACT) showed **statistically equivalent** tinnitus-disability outcomes to TRT (SMD −0.24, 95% CI −0.23–0.71, p=0.33). This supports CBT as an interchangeable first-line modality alongside TRT in Korean practice.

## Indications / contraindications
**Indications:** Persistent, bothersome tinnitus (chronic > 3-6 months), particularly when accompanied by sleep disturbances, high perceived stress, depression, or anxiety. 
**Contraindications:** Severe baseline mental illness needing acute psychiatric care rather than self-help app based CBT. Objective tinnitus or medically treatable underlying causes must first be ruled out.

## How it's delivered
Traditionally delivered in once-weekly sessions of 1-2 hours over 8-24 weeks by trained professionals in individual or group settings. Modern evidence supports **unguided digital/app-based delivery** matching the offline format via modular daily self-guided sessions (e.g., 8-week or 9-month durations as tested in Kalmeda and MindEar).

## Common confusions
**CBT vs. TRT:** TRT (Tinnitus Retraining Therapy) mandates the use of sound therapy in conjunction with specific directive counseling focused strictly on the neurophysiological model. CBT can be done with or without sound therapy and focuses heavily on broader emotional regulation, anxiety, and cognitive restructuring techniques (like challenging negative thoughts). The two can overlap in practice but follow different core protocols.

## App relevance
This therapy is the direct scientific justification for our app's [[wiki/app/cbt-program]]. App-based CBT has been proven highly effective in 9-month RCTs, giving us confidence to use this as the anchor of the app's efficacy claims.

## Open questions / contested claims
- **CBT without sound therapy for hyperacusis patients:** the optimal delivery when hyperacusis co-occurs is unclear — sound enrichment must be carefully calibrated, which pure app-based CBT cannot easily manage.
- **Effect size inflation:** the Walter 2025 effect size (d=1.38) is unusually large. The comparator was a waitlist control, not an active treatment; post-waitlist data complicates interpretation. No sham-controlled CBT trial for tinnitus exists.
- **Outcome instrument mismatch:** the pivotal app-based RCT ([[wiki/sources/walter-2025]]) uses the Tinnitus Questionnaire (TQ), not the TFI or THI. Mapping TQ effect sizes to TFI MCID thresholds is not validated.
- **Long-term durability beyond 9 months:** Walter 2025 shows sustained improvement through 9 months, but no data exist past that window for app-based delivery.
