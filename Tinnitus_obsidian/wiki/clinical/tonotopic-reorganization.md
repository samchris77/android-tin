# Tonotopic Reorganization

A form of maladaptive neuroplasticity in which the frequency map of the auditory cortex is redrawn as a consequence of chronic auditory deafferentation — neighboring cortical areas expand to represent the frequency region that has lost peripheral input, which may contribute to the generation or perpetuation of tinnitus.

## Mechanism

The primary auditory cortex is organized tonotopically: different neurons are tuned to respond to different frequencies, arranged in a spatial gradient. This organization depends on ongoing input from the cochlea. When cochlear hair cells tuned to a specific frequency region are damaged:

1. The cortical neurons previously driven by that frequency lose their primary input.
2. They become susceptible to lateral input from neighboring frequency-tuned neurons (because [[wiki/clinical/lateral-inhibition]] is also reduced via [[wiki/clinical/central-gain]] mechanisms).
3. Over time, adjacent frequency representations expand into the silenced zone.
4. This reorganized area shows elevated spontaneous activity — a plausible substrate for the phantom percept at the tinnitus frequency.

## Relationship to the tinnitus frequency

In many patients, the tinnitus pitch corresponds to the edge of the audiometric notch (the frequency region of greatest hearing loss). This is consistent with the tonotopic reorganization model: the boundary between intact and deafferented cortex is the zone of maximal reorganization and spontaneous activity.

## Relationship to other mechanisms

- Tonotopic reorganization is a downstream consequence of [[wiki/clinical/central-gain]] sustained over time.
- It reinforces [[wiki/clinical/thalamocortical-dysrhythmia]] by providing a persistent cortical source of abnormal spontaneous activity at the tinnitus frequency.
- Once established, it can persist even if peripheral hearing loss is treated (e.g., hearing aid use), explaining why some long-standing tinnitus is resistant to peripheral interventions.

## Therapeutic relevance

- **Notched sound therapy** — a variant of sound therapy that delivers broadband noise with a notch at the tinnitus frequency, aiming to stimulate neighboring frequencies and encourage lateral inhibition to reduce activity at the tinnitus frequency. Some evidence supports this as a mechanism for reverse tonotopic reorganization, but evidence is `low`. Not the same as standard TRT sound therapy.
- **[[wiki/clinical/vns-auricular]]** paired with multi-frequency sound — the VNS + paired-tone protocol used in animal studies (Engineer et al., Nature 2011) showed normalization of the expanded tonotopic map. Human translation is ongoing.
- Long-term [[wiki/clinical/habituation]] through TRT may coincide with some degree of cortical normalization, though this has not been directly demonstrated in RCTs.

## Open questions / contested claims

- Whether tonotopic reorganization is a *cause* of tinnitus or a *consequence* of chronic central activity is debated. Some animal models show reorganization without tinnitus behavior, and vice versa.
- The degree of reorganization measurable with current neuroimaging (MEG, fMRI) in human patients is variable and does not correlate consistently with tinnitus severity.
