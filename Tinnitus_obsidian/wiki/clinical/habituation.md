# Habituation

The process by which the brain learns to stop treating tinnitus as a significant signal, reducing emotional reaction and attentional capture — and, in many cases, reducing conscious awareness of the sound itself. Habituation is the primary therapeutic goal of [[wiki/clinical/trt]].

## Two types of habituation (critical distinction)

TRT specifically targets **habituation of reaction** first, and **habituation of perception** second. These are not the same:

- **Habituation of reaction** — the emotional and autonomic distress response to tinnitus diminishes. The patient still hears the tinnitus but is no longer distressed by it, sleeps through it, and can concentrate. This is the achievable, primary outcome.
- **Habituation of perception** — conscious awareness of the tinnitus decreases; the sound "fades into the background" and is not noticed unless attention is directed to it. This often follows habituation of reaction but is not guaranteed.

Confusing these — promising patients the tinnitus "will go away" — is a common and harmful clinical error.

## Neurophysiological basis

Habituation in TRT is grounded in Jastreboff's Neurophysiological Model: tinnitus signals generated in the auditory cortex are evaluated by the limbic and autonomic nervous systems. If the signal is consistently paired with neutral or positive context (not danger), the subconscious auditory filters progressively reduce its salience and route it away from conscious awareness. Key structures:

- **vmPFC** — evaluates the tinnitus signal and, once retrained, classifies it as "low-value noise."
- **Thalamic reticular nucleus (TRN)** — strengthened vmPFC inhibition of TRN closes the thalamic gate for the tinnitus signal, blocking its passage to conscious auditory cortex.
- **Limbic system** — reduction in limbic activation (anxiety, fear response) breaks the vicious cycle that amplifies tinnitus perception.

See [[wiki/clinical/thalamocortical-dysrhythmia]] for the oscillatory mechanism this process counteracts.

## Timeline

The neuroplastic remodeling required for tinnitus adaptation to reach habituation typically takes **6–18 months** with consistent TRT. This timeline must be communicated clearly to patients at the outset — failure to set expectations is a major driver of treatment dropout.

## Role in the vicious cycle

Habituation therapy breaks the vicious cycle:
- Initial tinnitus perception / hyperacusis
- → ↑ Anxiety
- → Negative emotional status
- → ↑ Sensitivity of auditory centers in brainstem
- → ↑ Tinnitus perception and hyperacusis (cycle repeats)

Directive counseling (reducing anxiety about tinnitus) and sound therapy (reducing contrast between tinnitus and background) work together to interrupt this cycle and initiate habituation.

## Common confusions

- **Habituation ≠ cure.** Tinnitus may still be audible when attended to; the change is in the brain's automatic evaluation of its importance.
- **Habituation ≠ masking.** Masking (covering tinnitus with louder sound) provides immediate relief but can *prevent* habituation by stopping the brain from ever processing the tinnitus signal. TRT sound therapy is set *below* the mixing point deliberately to allow processing while reducing contrast. See [[wiki/clinical/sound-therapy]].
- **Adaptation vs. habituation** — some patients adapt functionally (live with tinnitus) without true neurophysiological habituation. TRT aims for the latter.
