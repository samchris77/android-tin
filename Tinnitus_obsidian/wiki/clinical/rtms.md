# Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (rTMS)

## TL;DR

rTMS uses pulsed magnetic fields to modulate cortical excitability — low-frequency (1 Hz) stimulation over the left auditory cortex or temporoparietal junction (TPJ) is the most studied approach for tinnitus. Short-term effects on tinnitus severity are statistically significant but modest (THI −13 points, VAS −1.85 in meta-analysis). **Long-term (6-month) effects are not statistically significant.** rTMS must be combined with TRT or CBT to be effective; it does not work as a standalone treatment.

## What it is

A coil placed against the scalp generates brief, focused magnetic pulses that induce electrical currents in underlying cortical tissue. Depending on the frequency and pattern of stimulation, these currents either suppress (low-frequency) or enhance (high-frequency) cortical excitability. In tinnitus, the target is suppression of the hyperactive auditory cortex.

## Theoretical basis

rTMS for tinnitus targets the cortical component of [[wiki/clinical/thalamocortical-dysrhythmia]]: pathological γ-burst activity in the primary auditory cortex (PAC) or the TPJ, which processes and maintains the tinnitus percept. Suppressing excitability at these sites (via 1 Hz LFS) aims to reduce the neural synchronization and spontaneous firing that the patient experiences as tinnitus.

The mechanism parallels LTD (long-term depression) at the synaptic level: 1 Hz stimulation → MEP amplitude decreases over time → sustained reduction in cortical excitability. This effect is temporary without repeated sessions and without behavioral reinforcement (TRT/CBT).

## Evidence

**Strength of evidence: Low to moderate.** Individual RCTs show positive effects; meta-analysis results are mixed depending on time horizon.

- **Meta-analysis (He Z et al., Front Neurosci 2025):**
  - Short-term: THI −13 pts, VAS −1.85 (statistically significant, clinically meaningful)
  - 1-month follow-up: THI −11 pts
  - **6-month follow-up: THI −4.2 pts (95% CI −10.28 to +1.76) — not statistically significant**
- **Target site comparison (Watson N et al., Otolaryngol HNS 2023 — 19-study meta-analysis):**
  - Auditory cortex: ~50% responder rate
  - TPJ: ~100% responder rate
  - TPJ outperformance attributed to its role as a hub for auditory, attention, and emotional network integration
- **Combined rTMS/tDCS vs. others (Heiland LD et al., Otolaryngol HNS 2024 — 11 studies, n=1,186):**
  - Short-term: TENS and tDCS showed greater THI improvement
  - Long-term: rTMS showed greater sustained THI improvement
  - Depression: tDCS showed larger BDI effect
- **Safety:** Only mild transient adverse events reported (headache, dizziness, blurred vision, muscle discomfort). No serious adverse events in tinnitus trials.

## Standard clinical protocol

**Target:** Left primary auditory cortex (PAC) or left/right temporoparietal junction (TPJ)
**Frequency:** 1 Hz (inhibitory; LTD-like)
**Intensity:** 100–120% of resting motor threshold (rMT)
**Session length:** 1,000–2,000 pulses per session (1,000 pulses sufficient per clinical experience)
**Course:** 10 sessions over 2 weeks (daily)
**Maintenance:** Repeat courses at intervals of months as needed

**rMT determination:** The minimum intensity at which TMS of the motor cortex (M1) evokes a motor evoked potential (MEP) ≥50 μV in the abductor pollicis brevis muscle in ≥5 of 10 trials.

## Other rTMS protocols

| Protocol | Description | Effect |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| 1 Hz LFS | Standard; 900 pulses | Inhibitory (LTD-like) |
| 5 Hz HFS | Excitatory; less used in tinnitus | Facilitatory (LTP-like) |
| cTBS | Continuous theta-burst: 40s, 600 pulses | Inhibitory |
| iTBS | Intermittent theta-burst: 2s on / 8s off, 190s total | Facilitatory |
| PPS | Paired-pulse at specific intervals | Inhibitory or facilitatory depending on ISI |
| QPS | Quadripulse at specific intervals | Inhibitory or facilitatory depending on ISI |

## Indications / contraindications

**May be indicated:**
- Chronic subjective tinnitus not adequately controlled by TRT/CBT alone.
- Used as an adjunct to TRT/CBT, not a replacement.
- Actively used in Korean tertiary centers (including Pusan National University Hospital).

**Contraindicated:**
- Metallic implants in or near the skull (cochlear implants, aneurysm clips, deep brain stimulators).
- History of seizures or epilepsy.
- Pregnancy.

## Common confusions

- **rTMS ≠ ECT.** Electroconvulsive therapy uses high-intensity electrical current. rTMS uses subthreshold magnetic pulses that do not cause seizures (at standard protocols).
- **rTMS alone does not work.** Across the literature, standalone neuromodulation without TRT/CBT shows poor durable outcomes. The brain cannot reclassify tinnitus as low-salience through stimulation alone — behavioral retraining (TRT/CBT) is required.
- **1 Hz is inhibitory; 5 Hz and above are excitatory.** The tinnitus target is usually inhibitory (reduce PAC hyperactivity) — hence the standard 1 Hz protocol. Do not confuse with depression protocols (which often use high-frequency excitatory stimulation of DLPFC).
- **Short-term effects ≠ long-term cure.** The 6-month data does not support durable benefit from a standard 10-session course without maintenance treatment.

## App relevance

rTMS is a clinic-based procedure and cannot be delivered via app. However:
- App content should accurately describe rTMS as an adjunct option for patients who are discussing it with their clinician — not as a cure.
- If the app tracks treatment history, rTMS courses can be logged and correlated with THI trajectory.
- Patients using rTMS still need TRT delivered via the app — this is a key use case.
