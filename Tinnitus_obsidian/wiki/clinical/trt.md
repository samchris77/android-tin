# Tinnitus Retraining Therapy (TRT)

## TL;DR

TRT is the primary evidence-based management approach for chronic subjective tinnitus. It combines structured directive counseling with long-term sound therapy to induce [[wiki/clinical/habituation]] — training the brain to reclassify tinnitus as a non-threatening, low-salience signal. It does not eliminate tinnitus but aims to make it unobtrusive. Treatment takes 12–24 months.

## What it is

TRT consists of two co-delivered components:
1. **Directive counseling** — structured psychoeducation about tinnitus mechanisms, delivered by a trained clinician (audiologist or ENT). Not generic supportive counseling; it is mechanism-specific education based on Jastreboff's neurophysiological model.
2. **Sound therapy** — long-term use of sound-generating devices (wearable or environmental) to reduce the contrast between the tinnitus signal and background noise. See [[wiki/clinical/sound-therapy]] for device categories and protocols.

## Theoretical basis

TRT is grounded in Jastreboff's Neurophysiological Model of tinnitus: the tinnitus signal is generated in the auditory cortex but gains salience and distress through amplification by the limbic and autonomic nervous systems. Habituation occurs when the subconscious auditory filters stop routing the signal to consciousness. See [[wiki/clinical/habituation]], [[wiki/clinical/thalamocortical-dysrhythmia]].

The Triple Network Model ([[wiki/clinical/triple-network-model]]) provides a complementary framework: TRT works at the level of the Salience Network, teaching the vmPFC to tag tinnitus as low-priority, which breaks the SN→CEN attentional lock.

## Evidence

- **Strength of evidence:** Moderate. Multiple controlled studies and clinical series support TRT; a Cochrane review (2010) found positive effects but noted methodological limitations in the available RCTs. No large, well-powered multi-center RCT has been published as of this writing.
- **Effect:** Primarily reduces distress, not perceived loudness. [[wiki/clinical/thi]] score reductions of 15–25 points (clinically meaningful) are commonly reported in TRT cohort studies.
- **Limitations:** Long treatment duration (12–24 months) leads to dropout. Non-randomized studies are susceptible to selection and placebo effects.
- Evidence for the combination of TRT + neuromodulation vs. TRT alone is emerging but not definitive. (Shin SH et al., Otol Neurotol 2024)

### Korean HTA findings (NECA 2022)

The Korean government health-technology reassessment ([[wiki/sources/neca-2022-trt]], NECA-R-21-001-12, 2022-01) systematically reviewed 11 studies and reached the following conclusions:

- **Safety: HIGH (GRADE).** Only adverse event: ear-canal swelling/pain from a sound generator in one study — minor and comparable to hearing-aid/earphone use.
- **vs counseling or [[wiki/clinical/cbt]] (combined):** no statistically significant difference in tinnitus disability scores (SMD −0.11, 95% CI −0.41 to 0.20, p=0.49) or improvement rate (RR 1.01, p=0.66).
- **vs counseling alone:** disability scores not different (SMD −0.25, p=0.17), but TRT improvement rate significantly higher (RR 1.53, 95% CI 1.04–2.26, p=0.03).
- **vs cognitive therapy alone:** not different.
- **vs tinnitus masking:** **TRT significantly better** (SMD −0.74, 95% CI −1.16 to −0.32, p=0.0006). This is the strongest signal in the HTA and matches the theoretical claim that masking blocks habituation.
- **vs no treatment:** TRT significantly better (p=0.013 and p<0.001 in two studies).
- **GRADE for efficacy:** Very Low to Moderate.
- **Verdict (의료기술재평가위원회, 2022-01-14):** "조건부 권고함" — Conditionally Recommended for refractory tinnitus, as one of several effective options.

**Operational implication:** TRT's strongest evidence-based claims are *vs masking* and *vs no treatment* — not vs counseling or CBT. App messaging should reflect this rather than presenting TRT as categorically superior to alternatives.

## Indications / contraindications

**Indicated:**
- Chronic subjective tinnitus (≥6 months, or shorter if distress is significant).
- Tinnitus not responding to pharmacological management after ≥1 month.
- Best initiated before tinnitus becomes fully conditioned as a reflexive threat (early intervention preferred).

**Requires modification:**
- [[wiki/clinical/hyperacusis]] — sound therapy categories 3–4 require specialized protocols; standard TRT sound levels must not be aversive.
- Pulsatile tinnitus — medical workup must precede TRT; underlying vascular cause may be treatable.
- Significant hearing loss — hearing aids are first-line; TRT categories 2 and above require hearing aid integration.

**Not appropriate if:**
- Objective tinnitus with treatable cause has not been addressed.
- Active psychiatric crisis — stabilization first.

## How it's delivered

**Directive counseling content (Jastreboff protocol):**
1. Explanation of audiometric findings.
2. Basic anatomy and function of the auditory system.
3. Role of perception and contrast effects in signal processing.
4. Brain function and interaction with other neural systems.
5. Neurophysiological model of tinnitus applied to the patient's own presentation.
6. Detailed explanation of why and how habituation occurs.
7. Future treatment course: sound therapy use, expected timeline.
8. Q&A within the neurophysiological model framework.

**Session structure:** Initial session 1–2 hours; follow-up at 3, 6, 12, 18, 24 months. Delivered by audiologist or trained ENT clinician.

**Duration:** 12–24 months for neuroplastic remodeling to reach habituation. 6–18 months is the typical range cited in the literature (Henton & Tzounopoulos, Physiol Rev 2021).

**Cost (Korea):** TRT is **비급여** (non-reimbursed) under Korean health insurance — classification 소-4, EDI code MZ004, 보건복지부 고시 제2002-68호. Patients pay out of pocket at tertiary referral centers. Minimum required session duration per regulation: 30–40 minutes. Source: [[wiki/sources/neca-2022-trt]].

**Korean-market sound-therapy devices** (식약처 registered, 2021): Starkey TM-R, AMZ17ITC-DW-LP, Wave 2G, J-TRT, Ti-Masker. The NECA subcommittee noted that improved hearing aids are increasingly substituting for dedicated sound generators.

## Common confusions

- **TRT ≠ sound masking.** Masking covers tinnitus with louder sound; TRT sound therapy is set *below* the mixing point (the level at which the tinnitus is just audible within the background) to allow processing while reducing contrast. Masking prevents habituation; TRT induces it.
- **TRT ≠ CBT.** CBT targets maladaptive thoughts and behaviors about tinnitus (cognitive restructuring, exposure); TRT targets the subconscious classification of the tinnitus signal. Outcomes are comparable for distress reduction, but mechanisms differ. They are often combined.
- **TRT does not claim to cure tinnitus.** It aims for habituation — the tinnitus may remain audible when attended to, but the patient ceases to find it distressing or obtrusive.
- **Neuromodulation is not a replacement for TRT.** Neuromodulation (rTMS, tDCS, VNS) alone is ineffective. It must be combined with TRT or CBT to retag the tinnitus signal as low-salience. See [[wiki/clinical/rtms]], [[wiki/clinical/tdcs]].

## App relevance

TRT is the clinical backbone of the app. All sound therapy features, educational modules, and counseling content derive from TRT principles. Key app implications:
- Educational modules must cover the neurophysiological model at a patient-appropriate level.
- Sound library must support long-duration (≥8 hours/day) use at sub-masking levels.
- Progress tracking should measure distress with [[wiki/clinical/tfi]] (primary; treatment-responsive) and optionally perception with [[wiki/clinical/tmi]] (high-cadence companion). Avoid loudness-only tracking.
- Timeline expectations must be set explicitly — 12–24 months — to prevent premature dropout.
- See [[wiki/app/sound-library]], [[wiki/app/cbt-program]].
